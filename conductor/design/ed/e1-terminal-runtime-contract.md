## Shared lifecycle and ordering

At arrival, join ED-space queue keyed(arrival_tick,patientID); cohort includes arrivals waiting outside. Grant one free space then enqueue triage at that same tick. All first-care waits remain arrival->triage start, including outside wait. Space persists across clinical queues/work, boarding, pending transfer and transfer until actual physical departure. No automatic patient eviction.

Drain each tick's clinical/care/transfer completions and physical departures, arrivals and care-due notifications before allocation/grants. At treatment completion reveal decision using validated policy. Discharge intent schedules a distinct departure event at completion+delay, including same-tick event when delay0; drain that event before grants. Positive delay is a resource-free pending departure interval, with ED space retained and no fictitious discharge work episode. If due beyond horizon, EndpointState::DischargePending is unfinished, with no realized outcome/terminal/LOS. Admission sets boarding start and schedules first care due at decision+interval if enabled.

Grant waiting spaces after releases and before work grants. Apply finite-offer boundary effects and allocate exactly once per processed tick after all decisions/completions are known. Pending boarders with actively executing care are excluded from matching until care completes; their original decision readiness persists. This can miss an expiring offer and let another eligible patient go first; expose this policy consequence through trace/unfinished results. Do not reserve offers for active-care patients. Pass the complete eligible waiting set to OfferBook. An allocation consumes offer, closes offer-wait, enters pending transfer, cancels queued/due care (retain cancelled episode without completed work) and suppresses future care. Do not start transfer before resource grant.

After each actual care completion, if still boarding, next due=completion+interval. One care episode per patient at a time; no accumulation while care queued/active. IDs patient:boarder-care:index with index0-based; transfer ID patient:transfer. At a care-due/offer tie, allocation cancels queued care before any work grant. At care-completion/offer tie the completed care frees its resource and patient becomes eligible immediately. After allocation any stale care event is explicitly ignored based on lifecycle; it cannot enqueue new care.

Grant work in canonical(resourceID, queue_entry_tick,patientID,work-kind,index) order. Clinical stages and care sharing a resource use one FCFS queue; kind tie order clinical<care<transfer, only as final tie breaker (one patient cannot have simultaneous clinical and care). Existing clinical sampling remains queue-entry once and canonical patient-index based. Do not reorder events based on input vector order. Transfer completion releases work resource, performs actual departure and releases space once. Transfer resources never wait for space, preventing circular acquisition. Completions atH count; grants atH can have0 observed work; nothing afterH is reported completed. A zero-delay departure must not require a second OfferBook allocate at the same tick.
