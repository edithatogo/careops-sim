# Versioned urgency, priority and target policy

A common representation preserves classification differences. It does not assign
one universal clinical rank, hazard function or preemption permission.
This revision replaces the earlier draft's unsourced clinical mapping tables.

## Contract

Record scheme identifier/version, category code, jurisdiction/site policy,
source and effective dates, observation/reassessment time, urgency descriptor,
target definition and independent allocation/interruption rules. Targets define
clock start/stop/pause events, not only durations. Immediate targets need explicit
zero-time accounting; wait/target ratios are not clinical hazard estimates.

| Scheme family | Required profile qualification |
| --- | --- |
| Emergency triage (ATS/MTS/CTAS/ESI etc.) | Exact scheme and applicability; severity/complexity/capabilities remain distinct from urgency |
| Emergency surgery | Applicable local surgical system; do not substitute obstetric urgency categories |
| Obstetric escalation | Source version, clinical context and decision-to-incision versus decision-to-delivery definitions |
| Elective and outpatient waiting lists | Eligibility, referral/ready-for-care dates, pauses, reassessment, cancellations and benchmark denominator |
| Retrieval and transport | Service-specific urgency, capability/crew eligibility, dispatch and handover definitions |

Queensland's published 2017 emergency-surgery guideline uses A–E, illustrating the
need to verify current policy instead of using the earlier generic Cat 1–4 table.
Numerical targets and performance percentages require primary-source verification
in the selected jurisdiction/site profile before use.

## Safe scheduling

Queue priority and active-work interruption are separate decisions. Define safe
points, non-interruptible phases, responsibility handover and retained resources.
Preserve breaches as model outcomes; do not force care completion at the target.
Neither classification nor a numerical penalty is permission to interrupt care.

Urgent-care diversion must assess required capabilities, access/opening hours,
travel, patient choice and escalation pathways. ATS 4–5 alone is insufficient.
Frailty outreach and direct-admission pathways similarly require an explicit
eligibility and acceptance policy; no blanket triage bypass is assumed.
