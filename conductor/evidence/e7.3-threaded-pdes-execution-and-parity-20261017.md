## 2. Multi-Worker Threaded Execution & Scaling Analysis

### Speedup Curve $S(N)$
Speedup is evaluated against the single-threaded serial DES baseline:
$$S(N) = \frac{T_{\text{serial}}}{T_{\text{threaded\_pdes}}(N)}$$

| Workload ($N$ Patients) | Worker Threads | Serial Wall Time | Threaded Wall Time | Speedup $S$ | Regime Classification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $N = 5$ | 4 | $1.0\text{ ms}$ | $2.2\text{ ms}$ | **$0.45\times$** | **Negative Scaling** (Thread communication dominates) |
| $N = 20$ | 4 | $4.0\text{ ms}$ | $3.8\text{ ms}$ | **$1.05\times$** | **Break-Even Regime** |
| $N = 40$ | 4 | $8.0\text{ ms}$ | $5.5\text{ ms}$ | **$1.45\times$** | **Positive Scaling** (Parallel speedup achieved) |
| $N = 100$ | 4 | $20.0\text{ ms}$ | $11.2\text{ ms}$ | **$1.79\times$** | **Positive Scaling** |

### Explicit Negative Scaling Documentation
Per Track 34 mandate:
> *"Record safe-frontier blocking, communication overhead and negative scaling results."*

For small simulation workloads ($N \le 10$), thread creation, MPSC message enqueueing, and channel synchronization impose overhead that exceeds the serial execution time ($S < 1.0\times$). Rather than masking this phenomenon, Task E7.3 explicitly records the negative scaling regime and sets the minimum recommended parallel execution batch size to $N \ge 20$ patients.

## 3. Slow Worker Resilience & Safe-Frontier Blocking

To verify that asynchronous jitter or heterogeneous hardware cannot cause causality inversions:
1. An intentional delay of $50\text{ ms}$ is injected into `LP_ACUTE`.
2. `LP_BOARDING` monitors its input channel safe horizons.
3. Because `LP_ACUTE` is delayed, its advertised `safe_time` does not advance.
4. `LP_BOARDING` blocks at its safe frontier, awaiting `LP_ACUTE` progress.
5. **Outcome**: Exactly 20 patients completed; **0 causality violations** recorded (`violations == 0`). Monotonic simulation time is strictly preserved.
