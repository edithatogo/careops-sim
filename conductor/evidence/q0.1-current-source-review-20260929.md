## Technical findings

- DES and ABM still own separate legacy schedulers/worlds; `BehaviorSimulation` owns a separate `ABMContext`. No shared FlowRuntime, priority/preemptive ECS resource, or shared DES/ABM adapter exists in the reviewed source.
- `kairo-ecs-des` and `kairo-ecs-abm` depend on the shared core/types/state/RNG crates. An ABM-to-DES dependency is acyclic if DES does not depend on ABM. No new external dependency is needed for the proposed architecture.
- `Scheduler::schedule` increments its counters unchecked and wraps its `u32` event generation. `World::despawn` wraps slot generation. The proposed cumulative per-runtime `u32::MAX` schedule/create/despawn caps are sufficient only with fresh private state and every mutation mediated by the Flow facade. They do not establish global core/state overflow safety or memory capacity.
- `World::despawn` does not clean the separate `ComponentRegistry`; typed removal exists. Flow must remove internal components and cross-entity references before despawn, keep the registry private, and not claim panic-proof rollback.
- Read-only typed component gets are available. A new adapter must not expose mutable state or let `HashMap`/dense-store iteration order choose behavior. DES should own dispatch/handler routing; ABM should supply its adapter and agent-specific behavior through the checked boundary.
- Upstream core contains the release correctness fix that performs pending-event removal outside `debug_assert!`. The design does not modify or copy core behavior; current-upstream source is now the development base.
- Both DES and ABM gain public Flow symbols. The Track 25 inventory therefore registers both exact crate roots as experimental and keeps actual symbol/release review held.
