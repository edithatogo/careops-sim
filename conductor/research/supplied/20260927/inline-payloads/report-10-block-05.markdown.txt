## Decision

1. Preserve the current serial authoritative-state/clock semantics as the reference oracle.
2. Implement/evaluate independent replication parallelism before altering within-run scheduling.
3. Give each replication a deterministic stream namespace independent of worker/thread placement.
4. Define trace identity, state identity and statistical equivalence as distinct acceptance contracts.
5. Do not enable conservative within-run PDES until mutable ownership and cross-LP event envelopes are explicit.
6. Treat positive lookahead as unknown until proven from model semantics; zero lookahead is a required test.
7. Start within-run work with real shared-memory threads and synthetic partitionable fixtures.
8. Use coordinated safe-frontier checkpoints before considering asynchronous distributed snapshots.
9. Make stable event IDs, deduplication, worker incarnation and sender sequence part of the distributed protocol before selecting transport.
10. Defer cross-node within-run PDES until thread and localhost-process measurements show useful concurrency after synchronization costs.
11. Do not infer or promise speedup from theoretical concurrency.
12. Do not change queue/resource semantics or introduce cross-owner atomic bundles as part of this work.
13. Do not approve a third-party runtime/package merely because it is its latest release; current release, MSRV/toolchain, licence, platform and Kairos compatibility require a separate primary-source check.

## Equivalence policy

EQ_TRACE:
Canonical committed event trace is identical to the serial reference.

EQ_STATE:
Canonical state at agreed simulation frontiers is identical, while internal ordering may differ only under an explicitly reviewed independence/commutativity rule.

EQ_STAT:
Only predefined ensemble statistics agree. This is not sufficient to validate deterministic conservative PDES.

Initial within-run PDES acceptance target: EQ_TRACE on focused fixtures.

## Go/no-go criterion

A PDES backend is not promoted merely because it is correct.
Promotion requires both:
- required deterministic/failure semantics; and
- representative benchmark evidence that the additional complexity has a justified operational benefit.

A slower but correct experiment is a legitimate no-go result.
