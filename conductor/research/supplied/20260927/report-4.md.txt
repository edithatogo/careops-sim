# Mapping and parameterising a hybrid ABM–DES emergency-department model to clinical data standards and public datasets

## Executive findings

Research cut-off: **26 September 2026, Australia/Brisbane**. The target is an Australian public regional emergency department, with Queensland as the preferred operational context, New Zealand treated as a separate comparator, and international datasets used only where they add methodological value.

The main result is a **340-parameter register spanning all eighteen domains in the supplied hybrid ABM–DES scope**. Of those, 221 are classified as core, 39 as essential boundary parameters and 80 as optional or decision-dependent. A separate long-form mapping file contains **1,700 standards mappings**—five mapping families for each parameter—and a prioritised local-data specification contains **63 requested fields**. These counts describe completeness against the declared architecture, not a claim that every conceivable ED can be represented by exactly 340 parameters.

The most important substantive finding is that **the standards problem is considerably more tractable than the public-data parameterisation problem**. FHIR R4, HL7 v2, HL7 v3/RIM and OMOP can represent most patient, encounter, diagnosis, observation, procedure and diagnostic-event semantics. They are substantially less complete for dynamic queues, roster state, effective bed capacity, resource bundles, ambulance offload milestones, bed-request/bed-ready distinctions and latent behavioural rules. Those concepts need either narrowly defined operational extensions/source event tables or should remain explicitly outside the clinical data model as simulation configuration. FHIR R4 provides dedicated resources for encounters, observations, service requests, tasks and locations, including encounter status/location history, task execution periods and location operational status. citeturn1search0turn0search5turn1search1turn1search8turn1search2

The other major finding is that **Australian public data are excellent for demand, marginal case mix and validation targets, but generally insufficient for fitting the primitive process distributions required by a mechanistic DES**. AIHW reports about **9.1 million public-hospital ED presentations in 2024–25**, a national rate of 328 per 1,000 population, a median presentation-to-clinical-care wait of 18 minutes, and substantial differences in ED stay by disposition. Its public reporting also provides triage proportions, broad age/sex/remoteness distributions and admission proportions. citeturn6view0turn8view1turn8view2turn8view4 Yet the national collection itself is episode-level, while public-facing products are mainly aggregates; the current National Hospitals Data Collection specifications support much richer local or approved-data extraction than can be recovered from those public summaries. citeturn14search20turn6view2

Accordingly, the supplied public estimates are deliberately divided into three categories:

| Evidence class | Appropriate use in the model | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Public empirical input** | May seed a marginal or external boundary process, subject to transferability | National ATS marginal distribution; broad demographics |
| **Public validation target** | Compare against model output; **do not sample as a primitive DES delay** | ED waiting time, total ED length of stay |
| **Synthetic illustrative input** | Demonstrates schema/runnable configuration only | A 45,000-presentation regional ED, bed counts, illustrative rosters |

This distinction is critical. Waiting time and total ED length of stay are generated by the interaction of arrivals, resources, routing, queues and downstream blocking. Sampling them independently while also modelling their generating mechanisms would double-count congestion.

## Canonical model and standards crosswalk

A practical implementation should not attempt to make FHIR, HL7 or OMOP itself the simulation state model. Instead, source data should be transformed into a **canonical event ledger** from which DES state transitions and ABM observations/actions are generated. The key entities are patient, ED encounter, ambulance attendance, location stay, clinical task, diagnostic request/result, practitioner/roster shift, resource/resource state and inpatient episode.

```mermaid
erDiagram
    PATIENT ||--o{ ED_ENCOUNTER : has
    ED_ENCOUNTER ||--o{ LOCATION_STAY : occupies
    ED_ENCOUNTER ||--o{ CLINICAL_TASK : requires
    ED_ENCOUNTER ||--o{ DIAGNOSTIC_REQUEST : generates
    ED_ENCOUNTER ||--o| AMBULANCE_ATTENDANCE : may_arrive_by
    ED_ENCOUNTER ||--o| INPATIENT_EPISODE : may_lead_to
    PRACTITIONER ||--o{ ROSTER_SHIFT : works
    PRACTITIONER ||--o{ CLINICAL_TASK : owns
    RESOURCE ||--o{ RESOURCE_STATE : has_history
    RESOURCE ||--o{ CLINICAL_TASK : constrains
    DIAGNOSTIC_REQUEST ||--o{ RESULT_EVENT : produces
    INPATIENT_EPISODE ||--o{ WARD_LOCATION_STAY : contains
```

The underlying [Mermaid source is included in the package](sandbox:/mnt/data/ed_abm_des_standards_research/entity_relationships_mermaid.md).

FHIR R4 is the strongest of the requested standards for representing the operational event graph. `Encounter` carries the episode, status history, class history, period, participants, reason/diagnosis and location periods; `Observation` carries measured clinical state; `ServiceRequest` represents diagnostics, consultations and other requested services; `Task` can carry request/work ownership, priority and execution period; and `Location` can represent beds, rooms and other physical locations together with operational status. citeturn1search0turn0search5turn1search1turn1search8turn1search2 Australian implementations should preferentially reuse AU Base profiles and Australian identifiers/extensions rather than inventing parallel local structures; the AU Base R4 material includes Australian Patient, Encounter, Location and PractitionerRole profiles and Australian-specific search/profile support. citeturn17view0turn16search4

HL7 v2 remains highly relevant as a likely **source-interface standard**. The crosswalk therefore maps demographics to PID, encounters and patient location to PV1/PV2, diagnoses to DG1, allergies to AL1, investigations to ORC/OBR/SPM/OBX, procedures to PR1, medication events to the RX family, and participants to PRT/ROL where supported. PV1 contains patient class and admission/discharge timing concepts, while assigned patient location is represented in PV1-3. citeturn18search29turn18search12turn18search24turn18search13 However, there is no universal v2 representation for every ED operations timestamp. Existing installations frequently need site/interface-specific fields or Z-segments for events such as triage completion, handover completion, offload, bed request and bed readiness; these therefore require explicit local interface-profile documentation rather than inferred semantics.

HL7 v3/RIM is most useful here as a **semantic conceptual mapping**: healthcare events are Acts linked through ActRelationships, with patients, clinicians and locations represented by Entities in Roles connected through Participations. citeturn18search2turn18search6 It is less useful as the direct extraction model for a contemporary ED simulation than FHIR or the actual v2 feeds.

OMOP serves a different purpose. `PERSON`, `VISIT_OCCURRENCE`, optional `VISIT_DETAIL`, `CONDITION_OCCURRENCE`, `MEASUREMENT`, `OBSERVATION`, `PROCEDURE_OCCURRENCE`, `DRUG_EXPOSURE`, `PROVIDER`, `CARE_SITE`, `LOCATION` and `COST` provide a strong analytic representation of people, visits and clinical events. `VISIT_OCCURRENCE` includes admitted-from and discharged-to concepts, and `VISIT_DETAIL` provides finer-grained detail within a parent visit. citeturn24search23turn24search5 The OHDSI v5.4 change documentation is important because field names such as admitted-from/discharged-to changed from earlier naming conventions. citeturn24search14

A qualification is necessary on the user's request for **OMOP v6+**. The stable field-level OHDSI documentation that could be verified for this research is the v5.4 schema, and the machine-readable mapping therefore uses that documented schema as its field-name anchor rather than fabricating a v6 field map. The exact OMOP deployment version should be pinned at ETL design time and the crosswalk automatically validated against that release. The conceptual split—person, visit, visit detail, conditions, measurements, procedures, drugs, provider/care-site/location and cost—remains the useful modelling abstraction. citeturn2search3turn24search23

The principal standards crosswalk is:

| Simulation concept | FHIR R4 | HL7 v2 | HL7 v3/RIM | OMOP | Terminology / coding | Residual gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ED episode | `Encounter.identifier`, `status`, `period`, `class`, `location` | PV1/PV2 + ADT event | Encounter/Act | `VISIT_OCCURRENCE` | Local/national encounter codes | Minimal |
| Age/sex/language | `Patient` | PID | Person/Patient role | `PERSON`, selected observations | AU demographic value sets | Indigenous status and gender semantics need AU-specific handling |
| Presenting complaint | `Encounter.reasonCode`, Condition/Observation | PV2/OBX/local | Observation/Act | Observation/Condition source fields | **SNOMED CT-AU** preferred | Local presenting-complaint vocabularies may require mapping |
| ATS category | Proposed coded Observation/profile | OBX/local profile | Observation Act | Observation/source concept | **Australian Triage Scale 1–5** | No universally sufficient core field; AU ATS profile/value set is recommended |
| Diagnosis | Condition + Encounter diagnosis | DG1 | Observation/Condition Act | `CONDITION_OCCURRENCE` | **ICD-10-AM** and/or SNOMED CT-AU | Edition/version provenance essential |
| Vital signs | Observation | OBX | Observation Act | `MEASUREMENT` | **LOINC + UCUM** | Minimal when source coding is sound |
| Medication | MedicationRequest / Administration / Dispense | RX* | Substance administration Act | `DRUG_EXPOSURE` | **AMT/SNOMED CT-AU** | Workflow timing can be richer than OMOP drug exposure |
| Diagnostic request/result | ServiceRequest + Specimen + Observation/DiagnosticReport | ORC/OBR/SPM/OBX | Act + relationships | Measurement/Specimen/Procedure | **LOINC**, SNOMED CT-AU, UCUM | Order→collection→result→ack timestamps incompletely retained in many warehouses |

| Task ownership | Task.owner + execution period | PRT/ROL/local workflow | Participation | No direct native queue/task table | Staff-role vocabularies | Major OMOP operations gap |
| Ambulance milestones | Encounter/Task extensions/profile | EMS/local/Z-segments | Acts | Source extension/event table | Ambulance operational code set | **Major gap**: arrival, handover, offload, responsibility transfer and crew release must remain distinct |
| Bed capacity/state | Location operational status + extension/operational observations | Location master/local | Entity/Role | CARE_SITE/LOCATION plus external table | Local asset taxonomy | Nominal/open/staffed/equipped/compatible capacity not captured by ordinary clinical episode data |
| Queue priority/pre-emption | Task partly represents realised workflow | Local | Act attributes only partly applicable | No native representation | Model namespace | **Model-only structural rule** |
| Staff behavioural utility | No appropriate clinical field | None | No appropriate clinical field | None | Model namespace | **Do not force into SNOMED/ICD/LOINC** |
| Simulation seed/warm-up | FHIR `Parameters` only if exchange is required | None | None | None | Model namespace | Pure simulation metadata |

For Australian terminology, clinical concepts should be mapped to SNOMED CT-AU and Australian Medicines Terminology where appropriate; the Australian Digital Health Agency maintains Australian terminology releases and change information. citeturn17view2turn16search3 LOINC provides standard laboratory and clinical-observation identifiers and is conventionally paired with UCUM units. citeturn18search7turn18search19 ICD-10-AM should be used for the administrative diagnosis representation applicable to the data collection period, with the edition stored explicitly rather than assuming one code set over a multiyear dataset; current Australian materials already refer to Thirteenth Edition artefacts, illustrating why version provenance matters. citeturn15search19turn16search5

The principal proposed extensions are therefore narrowly operational rather than clinical: an **AU ATS Observation profile/value set**; ambulance queue-entry, handover-start, handover-complete, physical-offload, responsibility-transfer and crew-release milestones; dynamic Location capacity state with nominal/open/staffed/equipped/compatible dimensions; bed-request, bed-assigned and bed-ready events; and workforce availability/roster events. Queue ageing, pre-emption rules, utility coefficients, fatigue coefficients, target salience, random streams and other simulation constructs should stay in the model namespace rather than being given misleading SNOMED or LOINC codes.

The national data provide several useful starting constraints. In 2024–25 AIHW reported about 9.1 million ED presentations. Published ATS proportions were approximately **1.0% resuscitation, 18% emergency, 42% urgent, 34% semi-urgent and 6.1% non-urgent**; 30% of presentations were admitted to the same hospital. The displayed percentages are rounded and sum to 101.1%, so they should be renormalised before being used as a categorical probability vector rather than silently accepted as probabilities summing above one. citeturn8view2turn6view0

Published marginal demographics include approximately 49% male and 51% female among presentations; First Nations people represented 9.3% of presentations in the national reporting; 61% of presentations were by residents of major cities, while remote and very remote residents together represented 3.6%. These are national marginals and should **not** be multiplied together under an independence assumption to create synthetic patients. citeturn6view0turn8view1 A regional Queensland ED requires a locally fitted joint distribution—or empirical patient-level resampling—because age, remoteness, acuity, mode of arrival, presenting problem and admission disposition are clinically and operationally dependent.

For event-time parameters, the register specifies both **event stochasticity** and **parameter uncertainty**. A positive process duration may, for example, be represented as

\[
\ln T_{\text{minutes}}\sim \mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2),\qquad T>0,
\]

but only after a local fit demonstrates that lognormality is adequate. The uncertainty in \((\mu,\sigma)\) should then be propagated jointly from a bootstrap, fitted covariance matrix or posterior rather than resampling a new “mean service time” independently for every patient. Gamma, Weibull, empirical, mixture or non-parametric distributions should be retained when superior.

For categorical vectors such as ATS or destination, realised patients use a categorical draw while **uncertainty in the vector itself** is a different statistical layer, suitably represented by patient bootstrap, a Dirichlet posterior where justified, or a fitted multinomial model. For event hazards such as LWBS or clinical deterioration, a time-to-event model with censoring and competing risks is preferable to a single static probability whenever the event probability varies with elapsed waiting time.

The register also intentionally leaves **unknown numeric values as `not reported` rather than zero**. A zero means an observed or imposed absence; it is not an acceptable representation of a missing service time, unmeasured capacity or unidentified behavioural effect.

## Public datasets, fitted surrogates and evidence limits

AIHW is the strongest public national source for demand and performance calibration. Its 2024–25 reporting defines ED waiting time as elapsed time from presentation to commencement of clinical care, and ED length of stay as the interval from presentation to physical departure. AIHW also notes that a presentation can begin with registration or triage, which is a material measurement issue for simulation data engineering: both raw timestamps should be retained, and the reporting timestamp should be derived according to the applicable specification rather than assumed from one system column. citeturn8view3turn8view4turn8view1

The national 2024–25 figures include a median ED stay of **3 h 48 min (228 min)**, admitted median **6 h 13 min (373 min)** and non-admitted median **3 h 10 min (190 min)**. Corresponding 90th percentiles were **11 h 16 min (676 min)** overall, **18 h 57 min (1,137 min)** for admitted presentations and **7 h 44 min (464 min)** for non-admitted presentations. citeturn8view4

Where an illustrative distribution is useful for validation tooling, two published quantiles can define a **two-quantile lognormal surrogate**, not a genuine microdata fit. For \(T\sim\mathrm{Lognormal}(\mu,\sigma)\),

\[
\mu=\ln(q_{0.50}),\qquad
\sigma=\frac{\ln(q_{0.90})-\ln(q_{0.50})}{\Phi^{-1}(0.90)}.
\]

This gives:

| Validation quantity | Published quantiles | Quantile-matched surrogate |
|---|---|---|
| Presentation → clinical care | median 18, p90 118 min citeturn6view0 | \(\ln T\sim N(2.890372,\;1.467216^2)\) |
| Total ED LOS | median 228, p90 676 min citeturn8view4 | \(\ln T\sim N(5.429346,\;0.848072^2)\) |
| Admitted ED LOS | median 373, p90 1,137 min citeturn8view4 | \(\ln T\sim N(5.921579,\;0.869704^2)\) |
| Non-admitted ED LOS | median 190, p90 464 min citeturn8view4 | \(\ln T\sim N(5.247024,\;0.696703^2)\) |

These four distributions **must not be mistaken for fitted primitive service-time distributions**. They reproduce only two marginal quantiles under an assumed lognormal form and ignore mixtures, queue-state dependence, censoring and case-mix heterogeneity. Their proper use is in synthetic data tests, approximate graphical overlays or posterior-predictive validation of the final mechanistic model.

AIHW reports that **79% of ED presentations occurred between 08:00 and 22:00**. citeturn8view4 That one constraint is insufficient to identify an hourly demand curve. The included illustrative chart therefore deliberately uses a maximum-entropy construction within two time windows, with a synthetic 45,000-presentation annual volume: 79% is divided uniformly over 08:00–21:59 and 21% uniformly over the remaining ten hours. It is explicitly **not an empirical hourly fit**.

![Illustrative evidence-constrained ED arrival timeline](sandbox:/mnt/data/ed_abm_des_standards_research/arrival_pattern_timeline.png)

The numeric series is available as [arrival_pattern_timeline.csv](sandbox:/mnt/data/ed_abm_des_standards_research/arrival_pattern_timeline.csv). A real model should replace it with facility-level hourly counts and test NHPP, renewal, negative-binomial/overdispersed and clustered alternatives.

Queensland Ambulance Service provides useful prehospital boundary statistics. The official Jul 2025–Mar 2026 public performance indicator sheet reports statewide and regional activity and response-time measures; the statewide comparison column shows approximately **3,149 road patient transports per day** alongside broader incident counts. The sheet does not provide the patient-level ED handover/offload/crew-release distribution needed for the model. citeturn23view0 Queensland's public ED pages also expose current real-time hospital information, which can be useful for snapshot external validation but cannot replace a historical event log for distribution fitting. citeturn21search17

Health New Zealand's **National Non-Admitted Patient Collection (NNPAC)** is the appropriate NZ structural comparator. Health NZ documents the collection and publishes national file specifications/business rules, including non-admitted and emergency-department activity; however, public documentation should not be confused with freely available person-level records suitable for fitting an Australian regional model. citeturn21search3turn21search7turn21search15 Australian and New Zealand records should therefore be harmonised explicitly rather than pooled by similarly named fields.

MyHospitals is useful at the Australian facility/peer-group layer. AIHW provides a free API with JSON/CSV access and reporting at hospital, local hospital network, state and national levels; the current API metadata includes ED-oriented measure categories such as `MYH-ED`, `MYH-ED-TIME` and `MYH-ED-WAITS`. citeturn7view1turn11view0 This is valuable for external calibration/validation across hospitals but not for recovering the hidden clinician, diagnostic or bed-allocation service times that generated aggregate performance.

MIMIC-IV remains useful as an international methods-development comparator rather than an Australian parameter source. Version 3.1 covers source data from 2008–2022 and includes more than 200,000 ED patients in the source population; it is deidentified US hospital data requiring PhysioNet credentialling/agreements. citeturn24search21 The relevant DOI recorded in the source catalogue is **10.13026/kpb9-mt58**. Transfer of its service-time or behaviour distributions to Queensland without recalibration would be unjustified.

ABS-derived geography remains important for catchment, remoteness and socioeconomic linkage, but it does **not** provide primitive ED process times. QAS itself identifies ABS SA2 population estimates as an input to some of its population-denominator calculations. citeturn22search0 In the modelling ETL, patient geography should be linked to a version-pinned ABS geography/remoteness/SEIFA asset rather than embedding current classifications permanently into patient records.

Overall, only a small subset of the 340 register rows can honestly be populated from public national aggregates. The package directly source-seeds **15 register rows**; the remainder are local-data, structural, derived, calibration or optional parameters. This is a feature rather than a deficiency: fabricating hundreds of “literature values” would give a misleading appearance of precision.

## Prioritised local data request and identifiability

The most valuable next data asset is not another aggregate dashboard; it is a **pseudonymised, event-level longitudinal extract** linking ED presentation, clinical milestones, location state, orders/results, staff/resource availability, ambulance interface and inpatient bed flow. AIHW's national collection definitions should be used to preserve compatibility with national reporting, while raw source timestamps should also be retained because reporting definitions can differ from physical events. citeturn6view2turn8view1

The highest-priority fields are:

| Local data group | Essential fields / timestamps | FHIR target | HL7 v2/source target | OMOP target or limitation |

| Linkage/provenance | `patient_id_pseudo`, `ed_episode_id`, source system, raw timestamp, timezone/precision | Patient/Encounter identifiers, Provenance | PID-3, PV1-19, MSH/EVN metadata | `PERSON`, `VISIT_OCCURRENCE` plus ETL provenance |
| ED clocks | presentation, registration start/end, triage start/end, first clinician, physical departure | Encounter/status history, Observation, Task | EVN/PV1 plus OBX/Z as locally profiled | Visit start/end; intermediate clocks usually source extensions |
| Triage | ATS category, reassessments, assessor | Observation profile | OBX/local profile | Observation/source concept |
| Location | location event ID/code/start/end | Encounter.location + Location | ADT transfer/PV1-3 | `VISIT_DETAIL`, CARE_SITE/LOCATION |
| Diagnostic orders | order ID/code/time/priority | ServiceRequest | ORC/OBR | Clinical event plus source workflow |
| Pathology | specimen collection, accession, processing/result release, acknowledgement | Specimen + Observation/DiagnosticReport + Task | SPM/OBR/OBX | `SPECIMEN`/`MEASUREMENT`; acknowledgement generally external |

The complete 63-field specification, including priority, field definition, FHIR R4, HL7 v2 and OMOP crosswalk, is in [local_data_request.csv](sandbox:/mnt/data/ed_abm_des_standards_research/local_data_request.csv).

The **minimum linkage graph** should use stable pseudonymous keys for patient, ED episode, ambulance attendance, clinical order/task, location episode, practitioner, physical resource and inpatient episode. Direct identifiers should be used only within the approved linkage environment and replaced with research tokens before modelling. Australia's Privacy Principles regulate collection, use/disclosure, governance, data quality and access; the extract should therefore be limited to what is necessary for the declared modelling purpose and governed accordingly. citeturn19search15

Timestamp provenance deserves particular attention. At least four different notions can coexist:

| Timestamp type | Example | Modelling treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Physical event | Patient physically leaves ambulance | Drives actual state transition |
| Clinical responsibility event | Hospital assumes clinical responsibility | May drive clinical/organisational rule |
| Administrative event | ADT transfer recorded | Measurement layer unless shown to coincide physically |
| Reporting clock | Nationally defined presentation/start-of-care timestamp | Validation/output definition |

The model should retain all four rather than overwrite them into one “arrival” or “discharge” time. This is especially important because AIHW's presentation definition can begin at registration or triage and its ED-stay endpoint is physical departure. citeturn8view1turn8view4

Several important model components remain **unidentifiable from ordinary ED administrative records even with a good extract**. A staff utility coefficient, perceived patient waiting time, risk tolerance, team cooperation or target salience cannot be inferred simply because a corresponding action happened. Such parameters require either richer observational data, surveys/choice experiments, deliberate calibration against multiple behavioural observables, or structural sensitivity analysis. The register therefore separates the observed action from the latent rule that might have produced it.

Likewise, a fitted association between longer waiting and deterioration does not by itself identify a causal delay-harm function. Clinical deterioration modules should be introduced only where time-dependent clinical-state data and a defensible causal or prognostic specification exist. Until then, deterioration and safety effects belong in transparent alternative structural scenarios rather than arbitrary precise coefficients.

For service-time estimation, the local analysis should distinguish at least:

\[
\text{queue delay},\quad
\text{hands-on service time},\quad
\text{elapsed task time},\quad
\text{unattended observation/recovery time},\quad
\text{blocked-after-service time}.
\]

For example, laboratory “turnaround” should not be assigned as one lognormal duration if the same simulation also explicitly models specimen collection, transport, processing and result acknowledgement. Either model the aggregate turnaround or its constituent phases, but not both.

## Coverage audit, machine-readable outputs and implementation specification

The final package covers the complete D01–D18 scope supplied in the prompt, with **340 registered parameters**, **1,700 standard-specific mapping rows**, **35 explicit high-impact dependency relationships**, **63 local data-request fields**, a domain coverage matrix, evidence catalogue and a fully labelled example YAML configuration.

The domain-level audit shows where public evidence is adequate and where local data dominate. Public sources can anchor demand scale, broad temporal demand, several demographic and ATS marginals, admission proportion and aggregate performance targets. They do not identify the majority of the mechanistic ED parameters: hands-on clinician service times, effective capacity by shift, queue discipline, interruption/resumption behaviour, diagnostic phase times, linked ambulance offload milestones, dynamic staffed inpatient capacity, bed-request/bed-ready intervals, external discharge prerequisites or latent ABM decision coefficients. AIHW's controlled national collection infrastructure and public specifications demonstrate that richer episode-level definitions exist, but the public headline pages do not expose the event granularity required to estimate those mechanisms. citeturn14search20turn6view2

The recommended implementation hierarchy is therefore:

| Model tier | Include initially | Evidence standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Minimum viable core** | Time-varying arrivals; joint patient case mix; registration/triage; ED treatment streams; clinician/nursing resources; physical treatment capacity; core diagnostics; queues and resource allocation; admission decision; inpatient-compatible bed boundary; LWBS; measurement layer; simulation controls | Local event/roster/resource data wherever mechanistic |
| **Essential boundary** | Ambulance arrival/offload/crew release; inpatient access block; discharge prerequisites | Linked ambulance and hospital operations data |
| **Advanced ABM** | Patient perceived wait and leaving decisions; staff task choice; adaptation to crowding; bed negotiation; learning | Behaviour-specific observations or explicit structural scenarios |
| **Optional network/economic** | Interhospital transfer/retrieval, disruption scenarios, costs, utilities/QALYs | Jurisdiction/network data and matched IHACPA/local costing |

A model should be considered **parameterised** only once each core register row is in one of four explicit states: empirically estimated, deterministically defined by policy/architecture, calibrated with a declared identifiability limitation, or unresolved. “Default” should never silently mean “invented”.

For distribution fitting, a defensible workflow is to preserve empirical distributions first, test parametric alternatives only where operationally useful, account for truncation/censoring and clustering, and propagate parameter uncertainty separately from within-run stochasticity. Joint patient case mix should be represented by empirical joint resampling or conditional models rather than independent marginal draws. Dynamic capacity should be reconstructed from actual resource and roster state rather than treated as a Poisson random variable. Initial prevalent patients should use residual-time logic that accounts for length-biased occupancy rather than being initialised as fresh arrivals.

The strongest conclusion from the mapping exercise is that **clinical interoperability and simulation interoperability should not be conflated**. FHIR, HL7 and OMOP should preserve the facts that were observed—patients, episodes, locations, tasks, requests, results, diagnoses, procedures and timestamps. The hybrid ABM–DES configuration should separately preserve the generative assumptions—arrival processes, queue rules, capacity functions, behavioural utilities, hazards, intervention multipliers, random streams and uncertainty distributions. Linking those layers through stable IDs and explicit event semantics makes the model auditable without corrupting clinical standards with simulation-only concepts.

Conversely, the most important public-data conclusion is that Australia already supplies enough national data to establish **credible calibration targets**, but not enough openly aggregated data to claim a locally calibrated regional ED process model. The defensible next step is therefore not to import dozens of overseas service-time distributions. It is to collect the relatively compact, standards-mapped local event extract specified above, use AIHW/MyHospitals/QAS/Health NZ as external anchors, and then estimate the primitive distributions and behavioural models from the actual system being simulated.
