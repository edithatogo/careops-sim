## Corrected model semantics

### The joint case-mix problem

The principal structural error identified in the earlier specification was a factorisation in which downstream variables could be conditioned on **arrival mode without arrival mode itself being generated**. That is not a valid generative model.

The corrected predictive ordering is:

**facility/calendar state → arrival time → arrival mode → immutable demographics → presenting clinical state/visit type → triage/acuity assessment → service needs → orders/tasks/results and evolving clinical information → provisional disposition decision → downstream bed/discharge/transfer processes → realised disposition.**

The corresponding dependency graph in `dependencies.csv` was tested programmatically and is acyclic.

This ordering deliberately distinguishes **observed recorded attributes**, **latent or presenting clinical state**, and **downstream outcomes**. In particular, final principal diagnosis is not supplied to an arriving agent as if known at presentation. Australian national ED data define a principal diagnosis associated with the emergency episode, while the current NAPEDC specification separately records presentation, clinical-care commencement, episode-end and departure information. citeturn26view3turn27view3

### Planned versus realised disposition

Two disposition concepts are now explicit:

**Planned/provisional disposition** is an endogenous clinical decision state. It occurs only after sufficient clinical information has accumulated and can change as results arrive or the patient responds to treatment.

**Realised disposition** is an outcome. It occurs after competition among discharge completion, admission/bed availability, interhospital transfer, leaving at own risk, death and other applicable episode-end mechanisms. The current NAPEDC specification explicitly distinguishes episode-end statuses, including admission, completed care without admission, referral to another hospital, leaving after clinical attendance and death. citeturn27view4

Therefore, the published national admission proportion is **not** used as an independent Bernoulli draw at arrival when CareOps explicitly models the mechanisms generating admission. It belongs primarily in calibration/validation. The same principle applies to overall length of stay, boarding time, waiting time and occupancy.

For **clamped replay**, an observed disposition may still be fixed to reproduce an historical episode, but the package now requires it to be labelled as a clamped observation. A separate **unclamped predictive run** must generate disposition endogenously. This prevents replay information from leaking into claims about prediction.

### Kairos contracts remain unchanged

Neither of the two earlier architectural extensions has been silently incorporated:

| Candidate change | Corrected status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic acquisition of several resources | **Deferred / not adopted** | It extends the supplied current proposal of one capacity unit and one resource per work item. |
| New same-time event-ordering policy | **Review proposal only** | It must not supersede Kairos/KairoECS deterministic event semantics without an explicit design review and regression tests. |
| Suspend / Abort / Restart | **Preserved as supplied project proposal** | No new semantics have been invented for task classes lacking evidence. |

These distinctions are recorded in `parameters.csv`, `behavioural_rules.json` and `changelog.csv`.

## Evidence-backed initial parameter estimates

The reconstructed register deliberately separates four things that had a risk of being conflated: **published empirical values, policy values, derived arithmetic quantities and synthetic examples**.

Australian public ED data for 2024–25 report 9.1 million presentations nationally. The same source reports 49% of presentations recorded as male and 51% as female, with children aged 0–4 accounting for 10%, people aged 15–44 for 37% and people aged 65+ for 24%. These are useful initial marginal checks, but they are not a substitute for a joint patient-level case-mix distribution. citeturn26view0

For Queensland, AIHW's 2024–25 appendix covers 26 reporting EDs: seven Principal Referral/Women's and Children's hospitals, twelve Public Acute Group A hospitals and seven Public Acute Group B hospitals. This is a useful warning against treating a single statewide marginal as the profile of a regional referral ED. citeturn27view0turn27view7

The strongest immediately usable Queensland case-mix marginal is the published triage distribution for **Emergency presentations**:

The Queensland triage percentages are from AIHW Table A4 and apply specifically to presentations whose visit type was *Emergency presentation*. AIHW explicitly cautions that interstate variation may reflect different triage categorisation, different patient mixes, or both. citeturn27view1 The same appendix reports that 98.9% of the 9.1 million national ED presentations were classified as Emergency presentations. citeturn27view1

The **65,000 presentations/year figure has no evidentiary status whatsoever in this package**. It remains only because the follow-up specifically required it to be disentangled from evidence-backed defaults. The corresponding 178.08 presentations/day is merely \(65,000/365\); neither number is asserted to describe Cairns Hospital, a generic Queensland regional referral ED, or any other actual facility.

Likewise, the five primitive task-time examples are deliberately marked `synthetic`. No attempt was made to “back-calculate” them from ED waiting time or length-of-stay statistics. Doing so would confound queueing, blocking, parallel work, unattended observation and primitive active service.

### Published outputs retained as validation targets

Some public numbers are highly useful, but on the **output side** of the model:

|---|---:|---|
| Median presentation-to-clinical-care wait | 18 min | Validation target |
| 90th percentile wait | 118 min | Validation target |
| ED visits completed within four hours | 53% | Validation target |
| 90th percentile total ED stay | 676 min | Validation target |
| 90th percentile ED stay, subsequently admitted patients | 1,137 min | Boundary/boarding validation target |

AIHW reports these national results for 2024–25 and defines them as observed system performance, not primitive task durations. citeturn26view0

This distinction is especially important because the national data have non-trivial measurement exclusions. In 2024–25, AIHW excluded 14,415 emergency presentations from waiting-time calculations because a valid clinical-care commencement date/time was absent; 2,933 records had invalid ED length of stay; and clinical-care duration could not be calculated for 414,799 records because required timing measures were invalid or unavailable. citeturn27view2 These data-quality findings support retaining a distinct **measurement/observation layer** rather than assuming recorded timestamps equal latent physical events without error.

Queensland's quarterly open-data resource is also directly relevant for later facility-level calibration: its schema includes facility/HHS, triage category, attendances, seen-within-recommended-time percentage, median wait, did-not-wait measures, admission percentage, four-hour measures and leaving-after-treatment-started measures. It is a public CSV resource under CC BY 4.0. citeturn26view11 **I inspected the resource schema but did not inspect and extract facility rows in this bounded run**, so the reconstructed package does not invent or claim Queensland facility-level point estimates from it.

## HL7, FHIR, OMOP and terminology mapping

A new `interoperability_map.csv` contains **24 semantic mappings**. The design principle is that the simulation should have **one canonical CareOps event semantics**, with interoperability formats serving as source/target representations rather than determining the simulation ontology.

The canonical hierarchy in the package is:

**Australian METEOR/NAPEDC semantics → source-system event semantics → FHIR/AU Core or HL7 v2 representation → OMOP analytical representation.**

That ordering matters. The current 2026–27 NAPEDC NMDS is operational from 1 July 2026 through 30 June 2027 and defines the Australian reporting semantics for an ED stay; importantly, patients remain in scope until they have physically departed the ED even if they have already become admitted. citeturn27view3 This is particularly useful for modelling access block because “administratively admitted” and “physically departed ED” need not be the same event.

### Core crosswalk

| CareOps semantic event/field | Australian canonical source | FHIR/AU Core candidate | HL7 v2 candidate | OMOP candidate | Key caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Patient identity | Local identifier/IHI linkage where available | `Patient.identifier` | `PID-3` | `PERSON.person_id` | Pseudonymise before simulation use |
| ED episode | ED stay/episode identifier | `Encounter.identifier` | visit-number/profile field | `VISIT_OCCURRENCE` | Preserve source-to-simulation key mapping |
| Presentation | ED presentation date/time | `Encounter.period.start` candidate | ADT/PV1 event context | `visit_start_datetime` | Local Encounter-start semantics must be checked |
| Clinical-care commencement | NAPEDC clinical-care commencement | Explicit operational event; `Task.executionPeriod.start` only where semantically valid | Often workflow/Z-profile specific | **No lossless generic field** | Do not equate it automatically with encounter start |
| Physical ED departure | NAPEDC physical departure | `Encounter.period.end` or final location-period end if validated | ADT discharge/transfer event | `visit_end_datetime` plus raw event | Preserve the physical timestamp separately |
| Triage | ATS/NAPEDC 1–5 | Explicit coded priority/profile | Often PV2/local Z-field | Sidecar/Observation/source field | Generic “priority” is not necessarily ATS |
| Arrival mode | NAPEDC arrival mode | Local extension/Observation likely | Interface-profile specific | Sidecar/Observation | No universal one-field crosswalk |
| Current physical location | Local ED location history | `Encounter.location` + period / `Location` | `PV1-3` | `VISIT_DETAIL`/`CARE_SITE` | Need longitudinal moves, not final location alone |
| Clinician participation | Local provider assignment | `Encounter.participant`, Practitioner/Role | provider fields such as `PV1-7` where used | `PROVIDER` plus event relation | Visit-level attending clinician is insufficient |
| Diagnosis | ED principal/additional diagnosis | `Condition` / Encounter diagnosis | `DG1` | `CONDITION_OCCURRENCE` | Final diagnosis must not leak backwards into agent knowledge |
| Investigation request | Source order | `ServiceRequest` | `ORC` + `OBR` | Clinical event tables + raw workflow | Order lifecycle is not losslessly represented by result alone |
| Active work interval | Local task event | `Task.executionPeriod` when implemented | Workflow-specific messages/Z-segments | **Operational sidecar recommended** | Essential for primitive DES task times |
| Vital/pathology result | Source Observation/LIS event | `Observation` | `OBX`/`OBR` | `MEASUREMENT` | Observation, collection and issue times differ |
| Procedure | Source procedure event | `Procedure` | procedure-specific messages | `PROCEDURE_OCCURRENCE` | Operational substeps may not be clinical procedures |
| Indigenous status | Australian Indigenous Status | AU Core Patient extension | Local implementation | Source/Observation mapping | Preserve not-stated values and governance |
| Planned disposition | Local clinical workflow state | CarePlan/Task/extension depending system | Local workflow/Z-field | Sidecar/Observation | **Not the same as final disposition** |
| Realised disposition | NAPEDC episode-end status | Encounter disposition + explicit mapping | ADT/discharge mapping | discharge source/concept fields | Downstream outcome in predictive simulation |

AU Core v2.0.0 is the current published Australian profile set at the research date and is based on FHIR R4. It defines profiles including Encounter, Location, Observation, Patient, Practitioner, PractitionerRole and Procedure, and marks Australian Indigenous Status as a supported Patient extension. citeturn27view5

The map deliberately does **not** pretend that every Australian ED operational event has a native one-to-one FHIR element. Clinical-care commencement, task queue entry, active task start/stop, ambulance handover/offload/crew-release and provisional disposition are examples where an explicit CareOps operational event table or appropriately profiled FHIR resource may be required.

Similarly, OMOP CDM is highly useful as an analytical representation for encounters, visit detail, measurements, procedures, providers and care sites, but I would **not make standard OMOP tables the only ingestion representation for a DES**. That conclusion is an inference from the CDM structure: operational concepts such as queue-entry, eligibility, resource acquisition, suspension/resumption and arbitrary workflow-state transitions do not have a generic lossless task/event table equivalent. Retaining an Arrow/Parquet **operational-event sidecar** alongside OMOP is therefore the safer design. citeturn26view8turn26view9

The terminology layer in the package includes Australian ED diagnosis coding, AU Core/AU Base terminology and LOINC-compatible measurement mappings where appropriate. There is also an important version distinction: AIHW reports that the **2024–25** national ED diagnosis data used the ED ICD-10-AM **12th Edition** shortlist, while the current 2026–27 metadata specification has moved into the later coding era; these should never be merged without retaining coding-system version. citeturn27view1turn26view6

## Remaining evidence gaps

The audit changed the status of several seemingly ordinary “parameters” to **unknown**, because public aggregate data do not identify them.

The largest gap is **within-ED spatial and transit information**. A credible spatially aware hybrid model needs at minimum a versioned zone/room/bed graph, physical versus administrative location changes, origin–destination movement times, escort requirements where relevant, and potentially staff travel as well as patient travel. FHIR Location, HL7 visit-location fields and OMOP Care Site/Visit Detail can help identify recorded locations, but they do not provide the physical path network, walking distances or transit-time distributions required by the simulation. The package therefore leaves `EDP2-P022` and `EDP2-P023` unknown rather than fabricating them. citeturn27view5turn26view8

The other critical unresolved classes are:

**Primitive service times.** Active triage, clinician assessment, blood collection, imaging handling, procedures and discharge work need task-level timestamp pairs or time-and-motion data. National wait/LOS outputs cannot identify them separately.

**Effective resource availability.** Treatment bays, chairs, isolation rooms, equipment and staff require time-varying *open + staffed + equipped + compatible* capacity rather than nominal counts alone.

**Rosters and task permissions.** Provider role, skill, shifts, breaks, absence, redeployment and task eligibility need local data.

**Ambulance boundary milestones.** Arrival, handover, responsibility transfer, physical offload, crew release and vehicle-ready time must remain separate events. NAPEDC arrival mode alone cannot reconstruct them.

**Inpatient boundary dynamics.** Admission decision, bed request, acceptance, assignment, bed-ready time and physical ED departure require specialty/age/isolation-compatible bed capacity and competing non-ED demand.

**Dynamic leaving behaviour.** “Did not wait” and “left after treatment commenced” aggregate percentages can validate simulated behaviour but do not identify a time-dependent patient leaving hazard.

**Joint case mix.** The currently available public marginals should not be multiplied together as though age, acuity, arrival mode, time, diagnosis/service need and disposition were independent. Local microdata or a suitable controlled-access dataset is required for empirical joint resampling or conditional models.

These gaps are prioritised individually in `unresolved_evidence_ledger.csv`. The data request has also been converted into a 27-field/table specification covering encounter, location-event, task-event, order, result, roster, capacity, ambulance, inpatient-bed and spatial inputs.

The principal public-data choices are conservative. AIHW's current 2024–25 ED release supplies credible national performance and selected case-mix marginals, while its appendix provides the Queensland triage distribution and documents important data-quality limitations. citeturn26view0turn27view1turn27view2 Queensland's quarterly open-data schema can subsequently support facility/HHS calibration once its actual records are ingested rather than merely its metadata inspected. citeturn26view11 The current NAPEDC metadata standard supplies the preferred Australian semantics for the national reporting fields, while AU Core provides the current Australian FHIR profile layer. citeturn27view3turn27view5

The resulting package should therefore be treated as an **evidence-audited reconstruction and corrected specification**, not as recovery of the inaccessible original and not as a calibrated Cairns model. Its most important next evidence acquisitions are, in order, the original package itself for forensic comparison; patient-level joint ED event data; primitive task start/stop records; spatial/location histories; effective capacity and rosters; linked ambulance milestones; and inpatient bed-management events.
