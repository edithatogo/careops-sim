This is a curated, non-verbatim record of the ICU liaison evaluation discussed
in the project's November 2026 working conversations. Source documents remain
at their publisher URLs; no copyrighted report PDFs, conversation transcripts,
internal clinical documents, local CHHHS activity or private workbook are copied

## Verified public starting points

1. ANZICS 2024/25 Critical Care Resources and Activity Report:
   https://www.anzics.org/public/311/files/PDFs/CORE/2024_2025-CCR-Activity-Report.pdf
   Pooled report values: 20.4% of live ward/home discharges delayed at least
   12 hours (162 reporting ICUs); 18.4% after-hours discharges (167 ICUs);
   4.5% return ICU/HDU admission EPISODES over all admissions (168 ICUs).
   Cairns is listed in the rural/regional group. The pooled group includes
   non-Cairns and paediatric units and is not a Cairns effect estimate.
2. ANZICS CORE 2024 report:
   https://www.anzics.org/public/311/files/PDFs/CORE/2024-CORE-Report.pdf
   Adult Australian 2023/24 results include 4.0% patients readmitted during
   the same hospital episode and median ICU LOS 1.8 days. The APD patient
   denominator, cohort and time period differ from the CCR 4.5% episode rate.
3. ANZICS APD Data Dictionary v7 (October 2026):
   https://www.anzics.org/public/311/files/PDFs/CORE/ANZICS-APD-Data-Dictionary-Version-7.pdf
   Defines readiness/departure and other timing fields, subject to local
   completeness; requires separate linked hospital movements and outcomes.
4. ANZICS 2024/25 CCR report expenditure: about $1,524,354 annual reported
   expenditure per available ICU bed across a DIFFERENT reporting cohort;
   currencies and accounting basis require care. Do NOT insert this as
   marginal AUD cost of a released bed-day.
5. Study examples for economic structure: So, Yan & Chair (2019) post-ICU
   respiratory outreach (historical controls, US$145,614 reported savings):
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30595418/ ; UK NIHR/ICNARC critical care
   outreach matched comparison:
   https://njl-admin.nihr.ac.uk/document/download/2008763 ;
   Gupta et al. (2025) NP-led versus registrar-led MET cost analysis,
   an alternative staffing model NOT a routine liaison effect:
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.iccn.2024.103819

## Reconciled working decisions

- Evaluate a defined incremental service and comparator, NOT an unspecified
  generic ICU liaison role; distinguish discharge follow-up from MET response.
- Keep ANZICS CCR exit-block and all-admission readmission indicators for
  benchmarking, but separately define early UNPLANNED readmission per eligible
  live ward discharge for causal evaluation.
- Primary operational endpoint: index ICU hours between clinical discharge
  readiness and physical departure, with reason/amenability adjudication.
- Safety balancing: delayed escalation, ward arrest, unexpected death,
  readmission severity and appropriateness, and changes in ward burden.
- Cost boundary: added liaison costs and incremental hospital resources;
  cost-per-readmission excludes the cost of care otherwise required, and
  INDEX ready-to-depart delay costs exclude subsequent return episodes.
- Report gross resource benefits, net resource change and capacity separately.
  An occupied hour is not automatically a cash saving or a usable staffed bed.
- Parameter uncertainty and a no-benefit scenario are mandatory. Studies
  do not justify attributing their full effects to an unspecified CHHHS post.
- No direct clinical validation, patient-level extraction, deployment, rights
  approval or local economic acceptance has occurred.

## Local evidence gates

Before converting this illustrative scenario to an investment recommendation:
obtain the local eligible-discharge count, timestamp completeness and reasons,
early unplanned readmissions, linked post-ICU outcome/costs, proposed hours and
FTE, avoidable cost, and capacity-constrained unmet demand. Use approved
governance and an authorised private data route. No public upload of hospital
records, private budgets or literal conversations.
